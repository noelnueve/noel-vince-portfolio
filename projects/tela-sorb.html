TELA-SORB: Semi-Automated Machine for Sound-Absorbing Panel Production Using Textile Waste

Overview

Tela-Sorb is a semi-automated machine that converts textile waste into sound-absorbing acoustic panels, combining load-cell weighing, computer vision-based color sorting, and compression molding into a single automated pipeline—offering an affordable, eco-friendly alternative to commercial acoustic materials.

Quantifiable Results

  • 90–100% color-sorting accuracy across 150 test trials
  • Detection model achieved 0.995 mAP@0.5 and a 0.98 F1 score
  • Produces a uniform 7×7-inch acoustic panel from 160g of textile scraps

My Contribution

Programmed the core logic connecting the Arduino Nano, ESP32, and Raspberry Pi 5, enabling real-time coordination between the computer vision, weighing, and actuator subsystems, and developed the Python/C++ software for YOLO-based color detection.

The Problem

Tela-Sorb addresses two compounding issues: rising noise pollution in homes, small offices, and shared living spaces, and the growing volume of textile waste produced by the fashion industry—much of which ends up in landfills and takes years to biodegrade, releasing harmful compounds in the process. Existing acoustic solutions, including foam panels and fiberglass insulation, are often too expensive or impractical for renters and small-space dwellers, and few options address sustainability. Tela-Sorb targets this gap directly by converting discarded fabric into a functional, affordable, eco-friendly sound-absorption product.

Who It’s For

Designed for homeowners and renters seeking affordable noise reduction, students and remote workers needing quieter focus spaces, and content creators or small home-studio owners looking to reduce echo and background noise—all without requiring professional installation.

Additional Testing Highlights

  • Continuous operation testing showed the shredder could run repeated feed cycles with only occasional, easily recoverable clogging, confirming stable performance under sustained use.
  • Compression testing confirmed consistent panel thickness and durable adhesive bonding, verified over a one-month observation period.
  • Final panels measure 7×7 inches at 25mm thickness, sized for integration into a small-scale 3×3×5 ft cubicle structure used for acoustic testing.

Scope & Limitations

The system cannot process textiles containing metallic components, including zippers, buttons, and hooks, which must be manually removed beforehand. Color sorting accuracy can be affected by inconsistent lighting conditions, and the shredder’s capacity limits processing to small fabric scraps rather than large textile pieces. Output panels may retain minor traces of other colors due to residual material in the shredding mechanism. The system is intended for small to medium-sized spaces, not industrial-scale acoustic treatment.
